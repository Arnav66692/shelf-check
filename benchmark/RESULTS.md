## Failure reasons seen

Test A, A1: no failures. All 40 sampled products resolved to their exact NDC, all 40 labels were read, and every pair got an acetaminophen duplicate flag.

Test A, A2 (13 pairs with no numeric total): every miss carried the same app message, "the labels do not give enough to add up a daily amount". By dosage form (from the NDC file), 10 of the 13 are liquids, suspensions, syrups or granules, mostly children's products, and 3 are capsules or tablets. The A2 hits were mostly tablets and capsules (21 of 27). Tylenol's own label parsed in the 27 hits, so the gap is the sampled product's label wording. The API does not say which sentence failed, so the exact wording per product was not checked.

Test B, B1 (32 Major pairs with no flag). To explain these I fetched the openFDA label for both products in every missed pair (diagnose.mjs, 53 openFDA requests from this machine, cached in cache/openfda, output in diagnose-b-misses.json). This is diagnosis only and changes no number.
- In 31 of 32 misses, neither label names the other drug anywhere in its text, tables included. In the other one (disopyramide + potassium chloride), the disopyramide label says "potassium", but only about blood potassium levels, not a potassium medicine. So in 32 of 32 misses there was no label line naming the other drug for the app to find.
- In all 32, at least one of the two labels describes interaction risk by mechanism or effect instead of by drug name, for example CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, QT prolongation, CNS depressants, bradycardia or hyperkalemia. This is a keyword check, not proof that a given class line covers that pair. The app does not turn enzyme or effect wording into specific drug names.
- Of the 8 hits, by my reading of the quotes, 2 name the other drug directly (ketoconazole in the iloperidone label, levofloxacin in the toremifene label) and 6 match through a drug class word (MAOIs, sympathomimetics, antipsychotics or phenothiazines, antiplatelet agents, corticosteroids, magnesium-containing antacids).
- The Major pool leans toward specialty drugs, because the pool rule only needs one oral tablet or capsule in the NDC file. By my own hand classification of the drug names, 29 of the 40 kept B pairs include at least one specialty drug (cancer, HIV, MS, immune-modulating, or niche drugs like pitolisant, istradefylline and lefamulin). So B1 describes DDInter Major pairs in general, not the pills most people keep at home.

Test B and C, skipped pairs (3 in total): the app's name search returned only combination products for "Hydrocodone" (all Hydrocodone/APAP), and no single-ingredient match for "Sodium sulfate" or "Potassium bicarbonate". Each skipped pair was replaced by the next one in seeded order, as preregistered.

Test C, C1 (1 flag): dicyclomine + selegiline. The dicyclomine label's drug interactions section lists "MAO inhibitors" among drugs that add to its anticholinergic effects, and selegiline is an MAO inhibitor. On a plain reading the label does say it, so this is a class-level warning that DDInter does not list, not a parsing error. Per the addendum, C1 is an upper bound on false alarms, since DDInter's download has no files for groups C, J, N and others.
